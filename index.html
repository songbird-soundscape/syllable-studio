How it works

The spectrogram. Time runs left to right, pitch (frequency, in kilohertz) bottom to top, and darker means louder. A whistle is a flat line, a slur is a slanted one, a trill is a row of short dashes, a buzz is a thick smudge.

The practice birds. Their songs are built from notes, syllables and phrases the way real songbirds build theirs, and each sounds like a familiar North American bird. Because the computer made them, it knows exactly where every syllable is, so it can score your boxes: how many you found, how close your start times were, and how close your peak pitch was.

Why measure by hand? Automatic detectors still miss quiet, overlapping and unusual songs. Careful human boxes are how those detectors are trained and checked, and measurements from many people show how much measurers agree.
